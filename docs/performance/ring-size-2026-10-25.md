# Ring size study — 25 October 2026

How the Farm's ring size affects throughput now that tables are compact
(one ring word per entry in a uniform table, `27 + 8·SqCs` words of header),
measured to decide whether small rings are viable for occasional-use and
priority channels and whether the defaults should move. Ryzen 9 5900XT
(two CCDs, 32 MiB L3 each), Linux 7.2, LDC 1.43.0, Ant Farm `2.1.1-progress`
at `45a3a14` plus the minimum-ring and flush-wake changes recorded below.
Cache counters are `perf stat` demand or any fills from DRAM
(`ls_*_fills_from_sys.mem_io_local`), user space, whole process.

## Headline payload throughput

`tools/perf/throughput --once --nb 0 --body 1 --batch 1700 --ac 0
--constant-callandlength --n 64000000`, the PERFORMANCE.md headline cell
(tables of 1,700 one-word entries claimed 128 at a time), with `--ln` from
2^17 to 2^23 words. 16 threads is 5 producers and 11 consumers; 32 is 10 and
22. Medians of three to six interleaved runs; the spread between runs is
about ±15%.

| Ring | 16 threads, Mpps | DRAM fills | 32 threads, Mpps | DRAM fills |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 MiB | 2,699 | 170 k | 2,001 | 310 k |
| 2 MiB | 2,747 | 165 k | 2,110 | 305 k |
| 4 MiB | 2,369 | 46 k | 1,999 | 281 k |
| 8 MiB | 2,123 | 218 k | 2,139 | 274 k |
| 16 MiB | 2,500 | 195 k | 2,275 | 264 k |
| 32 MiB | 2,262 | 250 k | 2,161 | 249 k |
| 64 MiB | 1,748 | 224 k (demand); 2.0–3.1 M (any) | 1,822 | 248 k |

Two things follow. First, the ring's cache footprint is its live window,
not its length: 64 million one-word payloads flow through a 1 MiB ring with
the same 200 thousand demand fills as through a 32 MiB one, because the
region producers write and consumers read is a few tables wide and laps the
ring long before it ages out of L3. Rings from 1 to 32 MiB are
indistinguishable within the run-to-run spread, with the small rings at the
favourable end at 16 threads. Second, a ring larger than the L3 is slower:
at 64 MiB every lap lands on evicted lines, any-source DRAM fills rise ten
times (0.25 M to 2–3 M), and throughput drops about 25%. Huge pages removed
the 64 MiB ring's million TLB misses without recovering the throughput, so
the loss is cache, not translation.

## Fork-join, loop tables, Fibers

- **Fork-join harness** (`fwbench`, 32 threads, one run each): fib(39)
  100.0 / 102.1 / 102.3 / 103.5 ms at 1 / 2 / 8 / 32 MiB, skynet 47.3 / 48.7 /
  48.6 / 48.7, nqueens 122.2 / 123.6 / 122.9 at 2 / 8 / 32, matmul 60.6 /
  60.9 / 61.5. The dispatcher's deques never spill at the default
  capacity, so the ring carries only roots; its size is irrelevant. The
  1 MiB run needed `FWBENCH_SMALL_QUOTA=3072`: 33 producers at the default
  4,096 words exceed the `Exmax <= (K-1)·segCap` rule for a 1 MiB, K = 8 ring.
- **Loop tables** (`fwbench forkloop`, table mode, 8 threads): a 65,536-element
  loop at grain 16 (4,096 two-word entries, 64 KiB) runs in 7.0 / 7.3 / 8.7 /
  7.2 µs at 1 / 2 / 8 / 32 MiB; a 1,048,576-element loop at grain 1 (16 MiB of
  table) in 854 / 844 / 657 / 1,133 µs. A table that does not fit the ring
  is admitted in parts with `publish` retries, which costs about 30% at
  1–2 MiB for the 16 MiB loop, and a 32 MiB ring is again slower than 8.
- **Fiber warm burst** (`benchmark_mt 100000 shared 32`): 1.91 / 1.90 / 1.93 /
  1.94 M lifetimes/s at 1 / 2 / 8 / 32 MiB. Flat: Fiber activations are
  one-word tables of 32 and the Director's retirement, not the ring, bounds
  this workload.

## What the study changed

- `MIN_RING_MIB` is 1 (was 2). A 1 MiB ring with K = 8 leaves 16 Ki-word
  segments; what bounds a small ring is the quota rule, so a 1 MiB channel
  with 32 producers needs quotas of about 3,000 words or fewer. Sub-MiB rings
  would need the ring length expressed in KiB; nothing measured here argues
  for them yet, since the 1 MiB ring already costs nothing in throughput and
  about 1 MiB of resident memory.
- A Fiber lane on a `GroupHost` now wakes its consumers when a flush is
  refused and chain-wakes a parked peer after a written batch
  (`FiberReadyLane.noteFlushed`). Before, a 1 MiB ring hung the warm-burst
  benchmark at 20,000 tasks and a 2 MiB ring at 100,000: with the ring full,
  the worker whose flush was refused parked without waking anyone, and the
  parked peers' idle views pinned the segments that would have freed it.
  Large rings never lapped during a burst, so the defect never showed.
  `tests/ant_fibers/group_host.d` runs a 60,000-task burst through a 1 MiB
  ring with parked workers.

## Recommendations

Bulk rings do not need to be large: 8 MiB (the `FarmHost` and dispatcher
default) is right for tables up to a few MiB, and 16 MiB covers the largest
`parallelFor` tables; anything above the L3 per CCD (32 MiB here) costs
throughput. Occasional-use channels, a Fiber lane or a priority ring, are
fine at 1–2 MiB with quotas sized by the `Exmax` rule, which leaves most of
a 16 MiB L3 for the application on smaller hosts. The fwbench variants'
32 MiB default is a leftover from the payload-port days and should drop to
8.
