Ant Farm is a concurrent M:N mixed-type jobs distributor on a fixed-length ring buffer. The ring buffer is divided into discrete segments which subscribed consumers gain references to as they move across the buffer and release as work is completed. Segments with zero references can be reclaimed. Producers have a size quota measured in 64bit ulongs and publish sets of jobs in tables which can be one to arbitrarily many jobs long within their quota. Consider the sum of all quotas, the maximum simultaneous excursion of all producers *Exmax*. When a producer needs to refresh its quota, it checks the current and following segments to see if there's at least *Exmax* space. Jobs can be dispatched to exactly one (serial) or to many (parallel) consumers. A simple mechanism for granular work on a parallel job is provided.
The job tables have embedded cache-padded counters such that a shard of ~square root(consumers) claim one or several jobs in discrete chunks by incrementing their respective counter. The chunk size is dictated by the producer as the expected average time of a job. Consumer dispatch is structured such that some ensure full completion of tables, some opportunistically look ahead, some maintain shard balance, and some are given to each parallel job. Pains are taken such that *zero* contended compare-and-swap operations occupy producer or consumer work; the *only* CAS operations are in producer registration, segment initialization, and consumer unsubscription, and none of those entail retry loops. The end result is remarkably high throughput, low tail latency, and excellent flexibility.
Picture a farmer walking along a path placing down objects which a swarm of ants are picking up and taking away. He can't see the ants. He doesn't know how many there are. How can he avoid stepping on the ants? The answer: the ants maintain signage at regular intervals tallying how many ants are in the area. If it's higher than zero, the farmer waits. The last ant to leave a *confirmed-complete* area changes the tally (a reference counter) to zero. The last ant to leave an *incomplete* area instead leaves a pulse mark, so the farmer still sees a nonzero tally and will not step there. Thus the farmer only needs to watch for one signal to change, and doesn't need to try and communicate directly with any ants. And the ants have a panoply of strategies to break down and haul away their work pieces. Really the Ant Farm is a combination of known techniques and a disregard of FIFO guarantees; towards objectives of minimal synchronization upkeep, enhanced cache performance, and flexible role switching and load balancing.

A job is a callback function and a set of read-only parameters. The callback decodes the payload body as parameters to do some work, along with a pointer to the payload header and an iteration parameter which provides a discrete value for a parallel job's fraction of granular completion. Templates are provided as shims to generate callbacks and payload descriptions.
